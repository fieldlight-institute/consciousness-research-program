# Active Substrates: implementation and research status

November 10, 2026 · Public release

The program now contains executable synthetic studies, retained results, and explicit differences between the original design and what each study tested. The September specification and October 20 extension remain the design record. Their earlier “not implemented” labels no longer describe the whole program.

The strongest design distinction emerging from the work is between having information about a relationship, having authority to change it, and knowing which change improves the participants’ possibilities. The participant-authority experiment implements the first two; its chosen rule does not establish the third. That is directly relevant to generative participation: access and permission are necessary design dimensions to examine separately from the quality of the intervention.

## What has been implemented

| Design branch | Completed bounded work | Result and consequence |
|---|---|---|
| AS-01 static versus dynamic medium | Static records, diffusion, longer retention, and decay-only controls; 40 paired held-out seeds | Dynamic minus static completion: −0.114 percentage points, 95% bootstrap interval −0.465 to +0.244. This propagation rule did not establish a completion benefit. Its counted operation proxy is much larger. |
| AS-01 addressed information and adaptive edges | Source-aware reports and locally updated bounded spatial conductances | Addressed reports improve completion with substantial travel costs. Spatial learning is implemented; the tested learning rule has not established an additional completion benefit. These are separate comparisons, not a single ranking of architectures. |
| Extension A: recognition and authority | Local reliability estimates, permission to revise personal coupling, fixed and clock-driven controls, and independent-donor replay | Informed adjustment minus donor replay: −0.1327 points, interval −0.3729 to +0.1252. The estimate predicts arrival failure; it does not estimate the effect of changing coupling. Read strength and deposit strength change together. |
| Extension B: productive difference | Complementary-task feasibility and attainable-service checkers; whole-job reservation and bounded replanning studies | These establish machinery for comparing achievable service and respecting commitments. They do not complete the matched heterogeneous-versus-homogeneous population experiment. |
| Extension C: continuity through enactment | Earlier replacement/reset checks and related record/authority prototypes | The specified comparison of static records, repeated procedures, and revisable procedures across replacement and environmental change remains unrun. |
| AS-02 embodied recognition | Retained human-study design and conceptual account | No participant study is reported. The synthetic coordination results do not substitute for its bodily, experiential, or consciousness questions. |

## What the scheduling results add

Whole-job reservation and bounded revision each produced a +3.333-point completion difference in their own small deterministic comparisons, each with four wins and 36 ties. They use different workloads and comparators; the effects cannot be added together.

A subsequent replay of the saved revision schedules under a specified overrun distribution reduced its difference to +0.162 points, with an interval of −0.697 to +1.174. The plans did not respond to delays. The result identifies a weakness in tightly packed nominal schedules, not the outcome of an adaptive repair policy.

For one selected reversal, an offline schedule could still complete all three jobs, compared with two and one under the saved plans. That witness uses future information and freedom to assign work from the start. It distinguishes available physical capacity from what can be recovered after participants have committed.

Together, these studies sharpen the design problem: a system must preserve useful possibilities under its actual information, commitments, costs, and authority rules. Aggregate output in an ideal schedule is not enough to answer that problem.

## Remaining work in the October extension

**Recognition and revision:** separate prediction from intervention value; separate read and deposit changes; complete the time-shuffled, stress-exposure, and revision-latency comparisons. Keep the original factorial and donor results intact.

**Productive difference:** freeze a population-composition experiment with matched joint feasibility, resources, and task opportunities. The feasibility and reservation tools support this comparison but do not supply its result.

**Enacted continuity:** specify procedure-update and maintenance-cost rules before comparing retained records with repeated and revisable practices. Evaluate successor performance under changed conditions.

These are continuations of the existing research questions. No new experiment was run for this status reconciliation.
