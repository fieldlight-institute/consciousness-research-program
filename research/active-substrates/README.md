# Active Substrates: Memory, Coordination, and Consciousness

Author: Anni McHenry / Fieldlight Institute  
Created: September 29, 2026  
State: Research planning v0.1; not implemented; no results

What if some of the intelligence attributed to participants depends on the active properties of the medium connecting them?

This strand extends [Theory of Signal Becoming](../../THEORY-OF-SIGNAL-BECOMING.md) and the [Field Cognition Research Frame](../../FIELD-COGNITION-RESEARCH-FRAME.md). It investigates how persistent, responsive environments contribute to recognition, coordination and the integration of experience. A substrate can preserve a trace and also change the conditions under which the next participant encounters it.

## The common question and the distinctions it requires

Fascia suggests local mechanical responsiveness within a connected body. Crystalline materials make the effects of material organization concrete. Mycelial designs suggest trace, topology and accumulation across participants. These are sources of questions, not interchangeable mechanisms.

The computational track concerns measurable coordination. The embodied track concerns recognition and conscious access. The material track concerns physical transformations of inputs. Results travel between these tracks only through explicit predictions and comparisons. Useful computation does not, on its own, identify subjective experience.

A candidate contribution is coordination that preserves both cumulative capability and participants' ability to differ, refuse, or leave. Existing work on stigmergy and physical reservoir computing supplies essential baselines. Novelty is an open comparison, not an established result.
