# AS-02 — Embodied recognition and conscious access

Version 0.1 · September 29, 2026  
State: retained research plan; study design and collaborators pending

## Question

What role do distributed bodily and environmental processes play in a recognition becoming integrated and available to its subject? Can a person discriminate a change before being able to explain it, and which conditions improve or impair that discrimination?

This preserves the consciousness question directly. The larger hypothesis of a shared consciousness structure remains an open philosophical and empirical question; the first bounded study concerns access to information and the sequence of recognition, report and explanation.

## Retrospective preparation

Select dated examples of recognition preceding explanation from consented archive material, alongside ambiguous instances, reversals and non-events. Distinguish original timestamped report, later interpretation, and independently documented outcome. Use these to develop categories and candidate tasks, not to estimate predictive accuracy on the same selected examples. Private source material remains private; public plans retain only mediated descriptions.

## Prospective candidate design

A participant observes a controlled environment whose state sometimes changes. Randomize change and no-change trials with concealed labels; keep accessible sensory channels explicit. At predefined times collect forced-choice discrimination, confidence, bodily-description report and explanation separately. Record response times and task accuracy. Compare changes in discriminative performance with the timing and adequacy of explanation.

A participant can accurately distinguish conditions without knowing the mechanism. That would support a detection/explanation distinction. It would not identify fascia as the detector. Test bodily mediation separately only after defining a defensible intervention or measurement.

Physiological recording, if warranted, requires a measurement partner and an explicit prediction: which variable, at what latency, under what comparison. Include respiration, movement and shared-stimulus confounds when appropriate. Physiological synchrony alone cannot distinguish interpersonal coupling from response to a common input.

A proposed additional information channel requires a separate design specifying sender/input, receiver/output, ordinary-channel controls, randomization, blinding, timing, artifact monitoring and replication. Do not infer such a channel from metaphorical similarity or agent simulation.

## Requirements before any participant study

Choose the actual task; operationalize explanation scoring; decide sample size and analysis with a qualified collaborator; establish consent, compensation, withdrawal and data handling; obtain applicable ethics review. Link to the Institute's [human-subjects research work](https://github.com/fieldlight-institute/human-subjects-research-for-ai-systems). No recruitment or data collection is underway.

## September 29 revision — preserve the source hypothesis

The author's source proposes intentional pattern production: conceived pattern → embodied organization → changed interaction → returned response. It also proposes the human field itself as a sensing interface, with bodily processes as transduction and cognition as interpretation and intentional coordination. These are retained hypotheses, not findings. The narrower detection-before-explanation task is one investigation within this scope, not a replacement for it.

An embodied-pattern study must define the pattern operationally (for example, specified attention, posture, movement and breathing conditions), use an appropriate comparison, and separately measure discrimination, report and behavior. Improvements attributable to ordinary sensory, attentional or motor pathways are meaningful outcomes; they do not establish an additional field channel. The field-interface hypothesis requires its own identifiable input/output and controlled coupling test before a mechanism can be attributed.

The question of cross-system coordination also remains: what pattern can one participant deliberately produce that another can detect and transform through a specified channel? The relevant outcome includes a receiver's independent contribution, not only synchronization or compliance. No cross-species or physical-field experiment is specified or authorized here.
