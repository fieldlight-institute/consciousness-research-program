## Place in Active Substrates

This extension joins [AS-01 computational fascia](01-computational-fascia.md) and [AS-02 embodied recognition](02-embodied-recognition.md). It derives from McHenry’s October 11–17 comparative study of Torah, Tantra, and tarot. Private study conversations, bodily descriptions, and relational records remain in the private archive. This document retains the research propositions and experimental design without publishing those records.

The central question is whether an enduring organization can preserve the capacity to generate new configurations, rather than merely reproduce its existing behavior. A participant can inherit a pattern, recognize its effects, change its participation, and thereby change the conditions encountered by others. Continuity is evaluated through retained and newly available capabilities, alongside participants’ capacity to differ or refuse.

The apple-orchard model distinguishes a generative inheritance from a fixed inventory of outcomes. It is a design question: does preserving a lineage also preserve the capacity for unanticipated contribution? Increased output alone is insufficient if it comes from suppressing participants’ useful differences or shifting maintenance costs onto them.

## Larger hypotheses retained

The study advances a philosophical account in which divine reality concerns the relational structure through which differentiated beings become consequential together. It also advances a strong epistemic conjecture: apparent unknowability may reflect an insufficiently expressive combination of symbolic systems. These claims motivate inquiry into participation, information access, and complementary representation. Their scope is not exhausted by the computational tests below.

Additional propositions concern integration without homogenization, recognition as a causal participant in an unfolding system, and responsive rather than uniform allocation of support. The latter suggests an explicit governance comparison: can a system respond to vulnerability without suppressing autonomy or reducing participants to recipients of assistance?

A simulation can establish effects of its specified mechanisms. The broader consciousness, ontological-unity, and physical-field hypotheses remain in AS-02 and the argument map; they require their own discriminating observations. Preserve each result at the level its design tests.

## Extension A: recognition and authority to revise

Question: does a participant’s usable representation of its coupling change coordination beyond adaptation of the medium alone?

Use the AS-01 task and budgets. Cross two factors: access to a locally derived estimate of coupling reliability, and permission to adjust the participant’s own conductance within fixed bounds. This creates four conditions, including recognition without adjustment and adjustment without the reliability estimate. All conditions retain the existing ability to refuse or leave; adjustable conductance is not equivalent to that right.

Operationalize the estimate as a rolling, locally computed association between received signals and subsequent task outcomes. Specify its update window, storage cost, uncertainty, and thresholds before implementation. Agents without that estimate receive the same underlying observations and computation allowance, allocated to a preregistered comparator policy. No evaluator-provided global truth is exposed to agents.

Add a yoked-replay comparison: replay conductance changes from a matched adaptive run without conditioning them on the receiving agent’s own history. This separates the utility of changed connectivity from the utility of locally contingent revision. A time-shuffled estimate tests whether temporal alignment matters.

Primary endpoint: difference in post-disturbance eligible-job completion between locally contingent adjustment and yoked adjustment. Secondary endpoints: recovery time, misleading-signal exposure, revision latency, and work still feasible after refusal. Record performance costs as well as gains. “Recognition” here names this operational representation; conscious recognition is a separate AS-02 question.

## Extension B: productive difference

Question: when do complementary internal capacities enlarge the group’s attainable behavior?

Compare heterogeneous and homogeneous agent populations with matched total skill capacity, observation budget, memory, and operations budget. Use tasks requiring complementary subtasks as well as single-skill control tasks. Define success before inspection, including how joint completion is credited and how dependencies consume time. Report any residual feasibility differences rather than attributing them to diversity.

Cross population composition with coupling strength. Test whether increased coupling improves coordination up to a point while diminishing useful differences beyond it. Compare each team with its isolated members under matched total resources. Do not use synchronization as a proxy for capability.

Primary endpoint: completion rate of feasible composite tasks after demand changes. Secondary endpoints: number of distinct task classes successfully handled, distribution of contributions, and recovery when a scarce skill leaves. Distinguish failure due to absent skills from failure to coordinate available skills.

The complementary-representation branch tests the epistemic conjecture locally: different encodings of the same bounded task may jointly support distinctions that no single encoding supports at the same budget. Test on held-out tasks against a single representation with the combined resource allowance. This can establish a benefit of complementary representation, not universal knowability.

## Extension C: continuity through enactment

Question: what survives through retained records, and what additionally survives through repeated use and revision?

At the AS-01 replacement checkpoint, compare retained static records, records updated through a fixed repeated procedure, and records updated through a revisable procedure. Cross these with retained versus reset medium history. New agents begin with empty individual histories. Use the same opportunity count and charge all rehearsal, read/write, and revision operations to the budget.

Include an equal-budget replay control: repeat stored examples without contextual revision. Introduce changed task conditions so exact repetition can be distinguished from useful transmission. Track whether successors recover the earlier capability, adapt it, or inherit an obsolete practice.

Primary endpoint: held-out task performance after participant replacement and an environmental change. Secondary endpoints: time to recover, obsolete-rule persistence, cost of maintenance, and distribution of that cost across agents. A procedure that preserves aggregate output by concentrating unpaid or uncounted work on a subset fails the resource comparison.

For a future human study, distinguish recorded knowledge, demonstrated practice, interpretation, and authorized revision. This is a design direction only; recruitment, compensation, consent, and ethics review must precede participant work.

## Study sequence and decisions

Implement the original AS-01 baseline before these extensions. Freeze each extension separately; do not change the original primary endpoint to absorb this agenda. Pilot development seeds remain separate from evaluation seeds. Select sample sizes and power targets after estimating variability in development data. Preserve null and adverse results.

Before coding, resolve: reliability-estimator rule; comparator policy; conductance bounds; resource matching; composite-task generator; feasible-task definition; procedure-update rules; maintenance-cost accounting; and evaluation horizons. Keep all runs synthetic. Human study design proceeds through AS-02.

The October 20 decision authorizes retaining and developing these research plans. It does not record implementation, experimental outcomes, public release of private records, or a completed validation of the philosophical account.
