# Attainable whole job service

Synthetic offline evaluator extending the preserved complementary-feasibility oracle. No decentralized policy receives this information. Operations keep the earlier travel, simultaneous-work, handoff and nonpreemption semantics. A job is a nonempty set of operation IDs; jobs partition the supplied operations. Completing every operation in a job earns exactly one unit of service. A partial job earns zero. Operations cannot be counted twice or claimed by two jobs. Up to eight operations and eight jobs, four agents.

Cross-job dependencies are allowed. Every selected job expands to the transitive predecessor closure of all its operations before scheduling. This can require part of another job, or complete another job entirely; the latter earns its whole-job credit automatically. No dependency can be silently omitted to make an objective feasible. A separate schedule scorer checks dependency closure, validates the actual completed operations against the original oracle, and awards credit only to wholly completed jobs.

Enumerate all job subsets, canonicalize their operation closures, and rank candidate closures by their actual number of whole jobs. The empty schedule supplies lower bound0. Search from largest service count downward, using the earlier exact feasibility oracle. A feasible witness supplies a lower bound even if its minimum makespan is unproved. An infeasible closure is excluded only after complete feasibility search. Unknown or unsearched candidates retain their service count as possible upper bounds. Stop when a witness and all higher candidates establish the bound, or the shared node budget is exhausted. If higher candidates remain unresolved, return a service interval, never a fabricated exact optimum. Equal lower/upper means exact service count; it does not mean the returned schedule is the fastest among all equally productive subsets.

A global node allowance is shared across oracle calls. An optional per-candidate allowance allows a hard candidate to remain unknown while easier candidates establish a positive lower bound. Report nodes and tested/unknown/unsearched candidates; subset preprocessing and Python overhead are not included in those node counts. No exactness claim if the interval is open.

Correctness relies on the earlier oracle's bounded model. Any feasible schedule completes some set of whole jobs. The predecessor closure of those jobs is contained in its completed operations; removing other partial work cannot make it infeasible in this model. That closure is among the enumerated candidates, so exhaustive exclusion of all higher-service closures proves the optimum. This argument would fail for models where discarded partial work changes skills, resources or future feasibility; those are not implemented here.

Retain first demonstrations for conflicting independent jobs, unfinishable composites, cross-job prerequisites and incomplete search. Enumerate one-agent service bounds for three independent jobs of duration1–3 under common deadlines1–6 (162 instances); independently compute the subset-duration optimum as a check. This is a constructed accounting benchmark, not an empirical performance study or a resource-matched diversity experiment. Preserve all previous source/results and freeze this implementation before demonstration.
