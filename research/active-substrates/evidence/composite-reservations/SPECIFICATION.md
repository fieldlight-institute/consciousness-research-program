# Whole job reservation versus separate stage reservation

Frozen synthetic bounded comparison. Same two generalist agents, each X1/Y1, starting at sites0 and2; same observations, refusal interface and action of appending work to agent queues. No heterogeneous/homogeneous claim. Each seed produces three two-stage jobs, X then Y, at a single site per job. Release is uniform0–3; site uniform0–2; deadline uniform5–9; each stage requires1–2 ticks. All descriptions of a job become available at its release. Partial work is stored; handoff latency1 when the agent changes. Horizon12. Six operations fit the preserved exact oracle.

Both policies maintain their own committed reservations and may append work after already queued work. Neither may cancel or insert work into a previously reserved gap. Travel may begin after the preceding reservation ends, but cannot begin before the current decision tick. Future unreleased jobs are unavailable. Every tick, repeatedly consider released, unreserved work whose predecessors have reservations. Earliest finishing eligible agent is selected, tie agent ID. Scheduling considers predecessor finish/handoff, release, travel and existing availability; no global optimum or alternative-agent backtracking.

Stage policy sorts candidates by deadline, stage index, job ID and commits one feasible stage at a time. This permits first stages of equal-deadline jobs to precede second stages. Whole policy sorts jobs by deadline and ID, tentatively places both stages in order on copied reservation state, and commits only if the whole tentative plan meets its deadlines. A failed proposal changes no queue. Both retry unplaceable released work on later ticks; counters expose the different proposal cost. Whole reservation changes admission and grouping together; the experiment does not identify a pure effect of lookahead separate from that control.

Primary: paired whole minus stage completed whole-job fraction (three offered jobs per episode). Secondary: partial jobs, travel, work occupancy and candidate assignment checks. Evaluate each full workload separately with the earlier offline attainable-service optimizer, node budget100000. Retain lower/upper bounds and actual completion; assert every policy witness validates and never exceeds a proved upper bound. Do not divide by an unresolved or zero optimum. Offline advance knowledge makes this an attainable upper benchmark, not a policy's information allowance. Oracle nodes are not agent coordination costs.

Every committed stage is completed by its deadline; partially completed jobs receive no whole-job credit.
