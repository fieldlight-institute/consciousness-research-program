# Argument, evidence, and discriminating questions

Author: Anni McHenry / Fieldlight Institute  
Updated: September 29, 2026  
State: Proposed research; no new experimental results

## The argument being investigated

A responsive medium can contribute to the capacities of its participants by transforming inputs, retaining consequences of interaction, and changing future coupling. Intentional pattern production proposes that a participant can deliberately reorganize its conditions of access and interaction. The human-field hypothesis proposes the field itself as interface, bodily processes as transduction, and cognition as interpretation and deliberate pattern generation. A larger consciousness hypothesis asks whether participation contributes to conscious experience itself, including how distinct perspectives persist within a more extensive organization.

The computational, material, and embodied studies investigate different parts of this argument. A narrower coordination or discrimination result must remain connected to the larger question without being substituted for it.

| Proposition | Scholarly connection | Research obligation |
|---|---|---|
| A medium can actively shape the conditions its constituents encounter. | Langevin et al. (2011), fibroblast activity and areolar connective-tissue tension. | Separate matrix mechanics, cellular activity, and structure in AS-03. Do not generalize one tissue preparation to all fascia. |
| Material dynamics can contribute to computation. | Nakajima et al. (2015), soft-body reservoir computing. | Compare input-only and material-state readouts on held-out tasks; account for measurement and readout contributions. |
| Organization changes signal propagation. | Johnson (2001), photonic-crystal structures. | Specify material, geometry, signal, and transformation; test geometry changes rather than treating crystalline as a universal property. |
| A field can contribute causally to neural activity. | Anastassiou et al. (2011), ephaptic effects in rat cortical slices. | This motivates attention to coupling mechanisms at the demonstrated scale. An interpersonal interface requires separate evidence at its own scale. |
| Field organization may contribute to conscious integration. | McFadden (2020), cemi theory, a theoretical proposal. | Compare predictions with alternative accounts; specify what changes in conscious access, not only signal synchrony. |
| Interaction can contribute to meaning while participants retain autonomy. | De Jaegher and Di Paolo (2007), participatory sense-making. | Investigate reciprocal contribution and the ability to disengage. Agreement alone is an inadequate success measure. |
| Reproducing an embodied pattern may reopen access to a state. | Author hypothesis, connecting embodiment and intentional pattern production. | Define the reproducible relations and outcome before testing; distinguish discrimination, confidence, recognition, and explanation. |
| A human field could interface with a larger organization. | Author hypothesis; not an outcome of the above papers. | Identify candidate channel, input, transduction, response, and intervention predicting a change in coupling. |
| Shared organization may participate in experience without erasing individual perspective. | Author philosophical hypothesis. | Explain individuation and experiential access; distinguish shared information from shared subjectivity. Identify where competing accounts make different predictions. |

## Developing AS-02 without shrinking its scope

Three linked comparisons are required. First, compare deliberate reconstruction of a specified embodied pattern with a matched condition, measuring perceptual discrimination independently of felt familiarity. Second, if a candidate coupling channel is proposed, vary a parameter it predicts should matter—such as timing, orientation, distance, or selective interruption—while controlling ordinary cues and recording artifacts. Third, examine whether changes concern subjective access as well as task performance. Specify the first-person reporting procedure and how reports constrain analysis before examining outcomes.

Positive results in one comparison do not complete the others. Their value is that each constrains a different part of the same architecture. Results attributable to bodily, attentional, or sensory organization remain substantive results within the program. A finding inconsistent with the selected channel should revise that channel model rather than erase the research question.

Cross-system translation adds a further distinction: detecting a produced pattern, returning a transformed response, and retaining an independent choice are separate outcomes. Initial computational work can test these relations using synthetic agents; claims about birds, human fields, or consciousness require their own studies.
