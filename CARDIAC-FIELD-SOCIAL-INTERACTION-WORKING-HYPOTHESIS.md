# Cardiac Field and Social Interaction

Status: Working hypothesis 0.1  
Registered: August 19, 2026  
Program: Fieldlight Institute Consciousness Research Program  
Evidence posture: layered, falsifiable, and unresolved

## Research proposition

> Different internal physiological and emotional states produce different patterns in cardiac electrical activity and therefore potentially different measurable properties of the associated electromagnetic field; those field characteristics might correlate with changes in social interaction.

This is a **working hypothesis**, not a theory and not an established causal claim.

The sentence joins claims with very different evidence status. Fieldlight therefore treats it as a stack of separately testable hypotheses. Evidence for an earlier layer does not establish a later one.

## Claim stack

### H1 — state differentiation

Internal physiological and emotional states alter measurable cardiac and cardiorespiratory dynamics.

**Current status:** supported with important limits.

ECG, heart rate, heart-rate variability, and respiration can differ across induced or recalled emotional conditions. Rainville and colleagues found multivariate cardiorespiratory differences among fear, anger, sadness, happiness, and a neutral condition in 43 participants, while describing the result as preliminary. A larger meta-analysis of autonomic studies argues against treating emotion categories as fixed, universal physiological fingerprints.

**What would count as evidence here:** preregistered within-person classification above a declared baseline, replicated across sessions, with respiration, movement, medication, exertion, time of day, and participant-specific baselines represented.

**What would weaken it:** performance that does not generalize beyond the training session, disappears after arousal and respiration are controlled, or depends on one participant-specific artifact.

### H2 — biophysical measurability

Electrical activity in myocardial fibers generates a magnetic field that can be measured outside the body.

**Current status:** established measurement fact.

Magnetocardiography records these fields. Recent optically pumped magnetometer systems have recovered magnetocardiograms and spatial field maps from human volunteers, including in unshielded office environments.

**Boundary:** measurability does not imply social efficacy. A sensor detecting a cardiac magnetic field does not show that another nervous system detects or responds to it.

### H3 — social covariance

Cardiac or autonomic dynamics can covary across people during social interaction or shared experience.

**Current status:** supported, context-dependent, and mechanism-unspecific.

Dyadic and naturalistic studies report physiological synchrony associated with talking, cooperation, competition, proximity, familiarity, shared activity, and the sound environment. The same literature also shows why synchrony cannot be read naively as affiliation, mental-state identity, or direct influence. Shared stimulus, shared timing, respiration, body movement, task structure, and common environment can produce apparent or meaningful covariation.

**What would count as evidence here:** synchrony that survives time-shifted and pseudo-dyad controls and is reported together with the social and physiological context that gives it meaning.

**What would weaken it:** effects explained by shared stimulus timing, common movement, respiration, analytic flexibility, or multiple comparisons.

### H4 — incremental field hypothesis

Features of the cardiac magnetic field predict or explain changes in social interaction beyond what is already explained by ECG-derived cardiac dynamics, respiration, movement, proximity, shared stimulus, behavior, and ordinary sensory exchange.

**Current status:** open and unestablished.

This is the distinctive empirical question in the Fieldlight proposition. It cannot be inferred from H1, H2, and H3. It requires simultaneous magnetic, electrical, physiological, behavioral, environmental, and interaction measurement.

**Minimum positive result:** a preregistered magnetic-field feature makes a reproducible out-of-sample contribution after the comparison model includes the electrical and behavioral channels listed above.

**Disconfirmation condition:** well-powered studies repeatedly find no incremental magnetic-field contribution after those controls. A null result here would leave H1–H3 intact while rejecting or sharply limiting H4.

### H5 — direct field mediation

One person's cardiac electromagnetic field causally alters another person's physiology or behavior.

**Current status:** separate stronger hypothesis; unsupported by the evidence reviewed for this registration.

H5 is not required by the creator's proposition. It must not be imported into H4 through words such as *resonance*, *coherence*, *field*, or *synchrony*. Testing it would require controlled manipulation or attenuation of the proposed field pathway while ordinary sensory and environmental pathways are held constant.

## Operational definitions

### Internal state

Do not treat an emotion label as a ground-truth biological category. Register state through multiple channels:

- timed participant report;
- dimensional ratings such as valence and arousal;
- task or event condition;
- ECG or PPG;
- respiration;
- electrodermal activity where appropriate;
- posture, movement, exertion, sleep, medication, and stimulant context.

### Cardiac field characteristics

Candidate features must be declared before outcome testing and may include:

- beat-aligned magnetic waveform morphology;
- amplitude and spatial field-map features;
- temporal or spectral variability;
- changes from a person-specific baseline;
- coupling between magnetic features and simultaneous ECG features.

The magnetic signal must be distinguished from environmental interference, sensor motion, ferromagnetic artifacts, and cardiac-linked body motion.

### Social interaction

Use observable and participant-reported outcomes rather than an undefined global impression:

- turn-taking and interruption;
- speech timing and prosody;
- distance, orientation, gaze, and movement;
- cooperation or task performance;
- affiliation, trust, perceived similarity, or comfort;
- participant-recognized shifts with timestamped descriptions;
- third-party behavioral ratings where consent permits.

### Correlation

Predeclare the unit and lag: within-person change, between-person difference, dyadic synchrony, event-aligned response, or predictive contribution. A same-time association is not automatically interpersonal transmission.

## Research path

### Phase 0 — archive-derived hypothesis history

Use the Fieldlight archive to locate how the proposition developed across notebooks, chats, symbolic work, academic writing, and later formal language. This phase can establish provenance, recurrence, changes in formulation, and candidate observation windows. It cannot test a physiological mechanism because the historical corpus does not contain synchronized ECG and magnetocardiography.

### Phase 1 — within-person state study

Collect repeated sessions combining ECG or validated PPG, respiration, movement, timed self-report, and controlled or naturalistic state changes. Test whether person-specific and cross-person models classify state above preregistered baselines.

### Phase 2 — dyadic physiology study

Measure both participants' ECG, respiration, movement, audio timing, proximity, and social outcomes during declared interaction conditions. Include pseudo-dyads, time shifts, shared-stimulus controls, and correction for multiple comparisons.

### Phase 3 — magnetocardiography increment study

Add synchronized optically pumped magnetometer measurements for a technically feasible subset. Compare nested models:

1. context and behavior only;
2. context, behavior, ECG, and respiration;
3. context, behavior, ECG, respiration, and magnetic-field features.

H4 receives support only if model 3 adds stable out-of-sample information.

### Phase 4 — pathway test

Consider direct mediation only after a robust incremental magnetic result. Use distance, orientation, attenuation, shielding, blinding, and sham conditions designed with biomagnetism and experimental-psychophysiology specialists.

## Required controls and safeguards

- Preregister primary outcomes, lags, frequency bands, exclusions, and model comparisons.
- Preserve raw data and immutable timing records separately from derived features.
- Use participant-specific baselines before interpreting group averages.
- Measure respiration and movement; neither is optional in a synchrony claim.
- Include time-shifted series, pseudo-dyads, shared-stimulus controls, and negative-control outcomes.
- Correct for multiple comparisons and report null and contradictory results.
- Separate observation, participant meaning, statistical association, mechanism, and metaphysical interpretation.
- Do not describe synchrony as affiliation, healing, coherence, or transmission unless those constructs were independently measured.
- Obtain prospective consent for biometric, relational, audio, and identity-linked data, including a withdrawal and deletion protocol.

## Current decision

Register H1–H4 as a Fieldlight Institute working hypothesis stack. Preserve H5 as a clearly separated stronger proposition. Begin with provenance and low-cost within-person measurement; do not purchase or design a magnetocardiography study until Phase 1 and Phase 2 produce a credible preregistered signal and an external biomagnetism collaborator has reviewed the design.
